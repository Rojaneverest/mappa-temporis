# Phase 5 — line polish (2026-10-24)

Decision 58. These fix the problems in Rojan's close-ups: the fork around Alexander, the zigzag over Ceibal, and the era rule through San Lorenzo.

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Lanes and branches drawn with a multiply blend, so the colour doubled wherever a branch met its lane (the dark blocks at Alexander's fork) | Flat opaque ink in one colour; overlaps are invisible |
| Uncertain dates faded out in a gradient; clipped ends faded too | The bar ends cleanly (rounded) at the probable date and trails off in up to five dots towards the earliest or latest date |
| Archaeology lanes at 62% opacity with a dashed edge, so century rules showed through | A solid, lighter tint of the same pigment; legendary lanes lighter still, with a fine dotted edge |
| A thick zigzag across the whole chart at every change of time scale | A small double rule beside the year labels in both margins |
| Era boundaries an 18-unit dark rule that struck through labels | An 8-unit, 55% grey rule; labels and pictures keep a 34-unit clearance from it |
| Turning-point bands edged with hairlines across the chart | The shaded band only |
| Slid labels joined to their date by a loose curve, sometimes far across other ink | A square bracket: down the lane edge, a 45° corner, straight across; landmark labels reach out at most 1,100 units, others 600 |
| Conquest arrows up to 20,000+ units long, and thread-thin branches up to half a region long | Arrows over 4,000 units and branches over 6,000 units are not drawn; the events and the Folio carry those links |
| Threads as long dashed rules | Quiet lines of dots (round dash caps) |

What stays: native-script names on the lanes, lane width from territory, forks, rounded lane caps.

Effect on the numbers: the world sheet is unchanged in size. Labels without room are now P2 17, P3 153, P4 127; the era-rule clearance costs a few.
